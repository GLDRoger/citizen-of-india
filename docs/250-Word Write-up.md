Citizen is an independent redesign of how Indians could experience public services through UMANG and allied state services. It starts with a person instead of a department. One connected record, the Citizen Graph, holds identity documents, family, work, businesses, property, notices, payments, applications, permissions, and history.

Home turns that record into a daily brief: what needs attention, what is due, refunds coming back, documents expiring, applications moving. The request guide on Home understands plain language: type “Check my EPFO passbook” and it opens the right service.

My Records gathers documents, family, work, businesses, property, vehicles, and government history. Services lists what works for your profile. Benefits checks facts already on the record: age, bank account, residence, business type. It explains which schemes and loans fit, and what is missing.

Every journey in the demo works end to end. A citizen can check an EPFO passbook and raise a grievance, or reuse DigiLocker records to fix a PAN name. They can compare business loans alongside the money already due, pay a challan or property tax, file GSTR-3B, and finish shared work where both people give consent. Results flow back into Home, Documents, Money, and Activity.

Underneath the interface, one graph keeps facts, consequences, and permissions connected, so no service asks for the same details twice. Citizen works on a phone, in English, Hindi, and Kannada, and marks every government response as simulated.
