Citizen is an independent redesign of how Indians could experience public services through UMANG and allied state services. Instead of starting with departments, forms, and separate portals, it starts with a person and a Citizen Graph: one connected record of identity documents, family relationships, employment, businesses, assets, notices, obligations, applications, permissions, and history.

Home turns that graph into a daily brief. It shows what needs attention, what is due, what money is coming back, which documents are expiring, and which applications are moving. A plain-language request guide lets someone describe a situation and opens the right working service.

My Records gathers documents, family, work, businesses, property, vehicles, and government history. Services shows everything available for the active profile. Benefits evaluates connected facts such as age, bank access, residence, business type, registration age, and supporting evidence to explain suitable schemes or loans and what is still missing.

The prototype demonstrates complete journeys, not disconnected mockups. A citizen can review an EPFO passbook and register a grievance, reuse DigiLocker records for a PAN correction, compare business loans with current dues in context, pay a challan or property tax, file GSTR-3B, and complete shared tasks with explicit consent. Results flow back into Home, Documents, Money, and activity.

Citizen is advanced because the interface is only the surface. The graph underneath keeps context, consequences, and permissions connected. It replaces a catalogue of government websites with one understandable, responsive, multilingual public-service experience built around the citizen, for every stage of life and work in India.
